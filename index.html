What is love made of?

I’ve been thinking about the material properties of love. As in, how can we best characterize love in the way we characterize objects in our world?

Is it a rigid substance? If so, is familial love and romantic and platonic love all fundamentally made of the same substance, with different lived experiences giving it different compositions, different coatings and paints? Or is love more abstract, like a tube that allows different liquids to flow through it? Perhaps love is the facilitation, the space to create these exchanges rather than a thing.

Maybe love is an opening.

When I think about how I have experienced familial love, versus platonic love, is the fundamental emotion the same? The fundamental grounding for love is certainly shared experience - the ability to understand a human must come from a sense of alignment in some form, either through lived or communicated experience. I understand this through a popular idea in language theory - some set of mutual exchanges to arrive at something called a “fixed point” where two individuals can both assert that they mean the same thing. For example, two individuals from different countries and different languages might want to make sure they mean the same thing when one says the word “banana” and one says “banan”. This can happen through some set of actions where the terminal exchange and therefore understanding is reached when both individuals have convincingly demonstrated to each other that the other one indeed means what they mean. For example, one picks up a banana, one says banan, and the other banana. The exchange terminates with mutual agreement. The same identification could have been achieved if the individuals had together discovered a banana for the first time together, in addition to the shared connection over their full (live) demonstration of each other’s discovery.

Shared experience creates the opening for mutually understood communication. Is this the same as compatibility or alignment? I will perhaps never share the same views or perspectives as my family, on most things - my parents grew up in the Soviet Union and my brother in Russia. Their experiences of life are painted so fundamentally differently than mine, an American born and raised in the Florida Keys. Structurally, our life experiences in every realm - but perhaps familial - have coated our general perspectives to be very different. Yet the feeling itself does seem to come from an understanding of their experience, or at least an openness to it.

The love I feel for my family feels deeply weighed, weighed with years of baggage and shared experience and injected with pain and judgement. It feels heavy, and intimate in a very different way than platonic love. But the fundamental feeling of familiarity, the feeling that falls beneath all of the noise and weight - that might be the same for platonic and familial.

Maybe we have different substrates flowing through the opening created by our familial love and romantic love, which makes our experience of love so different. But the fundamental construction of these experiences is rooted in the same opening, jarred, hammered, and carved open through different ways for each relationship.
